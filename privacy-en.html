Privacy Policy

Aguantín app (com.jrsoft.bateria) · Last updated: 17 November 2026

In one sentence: this app has no user accounts and no server, and your battery readings, charging history, charger tests and settings never leave your phone (unless you move them to a new phone yourself or export your history). Only advertising sends data, and it is Google's library that does it.

The Spanish version is the reference version of this policy; this is a translation of the same version.

Who is responsible

The data controller is JR Soft, trade name of Javier Román Sáez, the app's developer. You can contact us at demual@gmail.com.

What this app does NOT do

  • There is no sign-up, account, user profile or server of our own. The app runs entirely on your phone.
  • There is no analytics or crash-reporting tool of our own: no Google Analytics, no Crashlytics, nothing similar. The only library that sends data of its own to a third party is the advertising one, explained below. The app includes the Firebase remote configuration library (Remote Config), which could be used to adjust advertising without publishing a new version, but it is switched off: it does not start and does not connect to any server. If it is ever switched on, this policy will be updated first.
  • It does not use your phone's location, camera, microphone, contacts, photos or files. Reading the battery needs no permission: Android gives that information to any app.
  • It does not look at which other apps you have or how much each one uses.
  • JR Soft does not keep your data, except the emails you send us, and does not hand lists or databases of it to anyone. To show and measure ads, Google's advertising SDK (AdMob) collects device identifiers and other technical data on its own and sends them to Google, as explained in this policy. Some US privacy laws consider letting a third party's SDK collect that data for personalised advertising to be "selling" or "sharing" personal data; if you live there, you can opt out (see "US residents").

Data that stays only on your phone

This information is stored in the app's private storage and does not leave it. It is deleted completely if you uninstall the app or clear its data in Android settings (Settings → Apps → Aguantín → Storage → Clear data).

  • Battery readings: date and time, level, whether it is charging, the charger type (AC, USB or wireless), temperature, voltage, approximate current and the phone's charge counter. With the app closed they are taken in the background, about every 15 minutes and more often while charging.
  • Your charges: when each one started and ended, from which level to which, with which charger, the highest temperature and current, and how much went in.
  • Estimated health: the capacity the app calculates from your charges, the cycles (those reported by Android 14 or later, if the manufacturer fills them in, and those counted by the app) and the design capacity, only if you type it in.
  • Your charger tests: the name you give them and what each one measured (average current and power, voltage, level and duration). You can delete them one by one in the app.
  • Your settings: style, theme, temperature unit, which alerts you want and at what level or temperature, and whether you want the status notification.
  • What the app learns about this phone: the unit and sign it uses for current, the charge limit it has been seen to stop at (for example 80%) and which alerts have already been sent, so they are not repeated.
  • The date you were last asked to rate the app, so it is not asked more than once every 120 days.
  • The phone's manufacturer and model, which the Guide checks at that moment to show you the steps for your brand. They are not stored or sent.
  • The country of your SIM card (or, if there is none, the system region), which the "More apps by JR Soft" screen checks at that moment to show only the apps available in your country. It is not stored or sent. The Google Play links on that screen and in "Share the app" carry a tag saying they come from this app, with none of your data.

Alerts, widgets and the quick settings tile are generated on the phone itself from this data. None of them goes through a server: there are no notifications sent over the internet. Widgets show your battery level on the home screen, visible to anyone who looks at the phone.

Exporting your history

If you tap "Export history (CSV)", the app creates a file on the phone with your readings and charges, and Android lets you choose which app to send it to (your email, your cloud storage, a messaging app…). You send it, and only where you choose; from then on, that app's privacy terms apply. JR Soft receives nothing.

Backups, moving to a new phone and what is not transferred

Android cloud backup (the one in your Google account) is switched off for this app's data: none of it is uploaded. Android does keep the list of apps you had installed in your account, so it can reinstall them, but Google manages that list and it contains no app data.

If, when setting up a new phone, you copy your data directly from the old one, with or without a cable, your history, charges, charger tests and settings may move to the new phone. You ask for that transfer and Android carries it out, with its own tool or the phone maker's, under their own terms. According to Android's documentation, it goes from one phone to the other without passing through servers. It never passes through ours: JR Soft receives nothing. Whether it happens depends on the Android version and the manufacturer, so we cannot guarantee it. On Android 7 and 8 nothing is transferred.

What only makes sense on the old phone is not transferred: what the app learned about that phone (the current unit, its charge limit and the alerts already sent), your advertising choice and the advertising's internal state. On the new phone, the app will ask you again for whatever is needed.

Data that does leave your phone

Advertising: Google AdMob

The app shows discreet advertising served by Google's advertising SDK (AdMob): a banner at the bottom of the History and Health screens, and native ads, labelled "Ad", between the charges in the history and at the end of a charge's detail. There are never full-screen ads, nor ads on the battery status, the Guide, alerts, widgets or settings. For advertising, the app declares the com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID permission, which gives access to Android's advertising ID; you can reset or delete this ID in Android Settings → Google → Ads (or Settings → Privacy → Ads, depending on the phone).

Besides the advertising ID, the AdMob SDK collects the app set ID, interactions with the app and with ads, and diagnostic data (launch time, hangs and energy use). Google may use your IP address to estimate an approximate location, roughly at city level. Google uses this data to serve and measure advertising, for statistics and to prevent fraud, under its own policy. On phones that have them, the SDK may also use Android's advertising features (Privacy Sandbox), which you can manage in Android's privacy settings.

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, and wherever the law requires it, the app shows Google's consent form (UMP) before serving personalised advertising. You can change your choice at any time in Settings → Ad privacy options.

Google shows the ad consent message with text common to all its advertising partners, which is why it mentions precise geolocation. According to Google, its ads SDK only uses the approximate location inferred from the IP address, and this app does not give it any location. You can refuse that use under "Manage options".

Your battery data never reaches advertising. Ad requests are sent without any of your readings, charges or settings. If you refuse consent, you will see non-personalised or limited ads and the app will work exactly the same: no feature is locked.

Rating, sharing and writing to us

If you rate the app, the rating window belongs to Google Play and Google receives the rating with your account, under its policy. "Share the app" hands a link to the app you choose. If you write to us with "Contact us", you send the email from your own email app and we receive your address and what you tell us.

IP address

As with any app that connects to the internet, Google's servers receive your IP address when ads are requested. The app does not connect to any other server.

Transfers outside Europe

Google (AdMob) may process data outside the European Economic Area. Google LLC participates in the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, its UK Extension and the Swiss-US framework.

Legal basis and retention

Legal basis. What is processed on your phone does not reach JR Soft: your readings, charges, tests and settings are stored so the app can do what you ask it to (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). For what does leave it: (1) personalised advertising and use of device storage for advertising purposes in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland: your consent, which you give or refuse in Google's form and can withdraw in Settings → Ad privacy options (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR and Art. 22(2) of the Spanish LSSI-CE); (2) non-personalised advertising, its measurement, technical logs and fraud prevention: the legitimate interest in funding a free app, keeping it working well and protecting it from fraud (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR); (3) if you write to us: the legitimate interest in replying and solving what you tell us (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). Google processes AdMob data as an independent controller, under its own policies.

Data stored on your phone stays there until you delete it, uninstall the app or clear its data. The history has no day limit: it grows slowly, because it keeps at most one reading every 4 minutes. This app keeps no data outside your phone, except the messages you send us, which are deleted once the matter is resolved and at most one year after we receive them; Google keeps its own data under its policies.

Security

What the app stores is in its private storage, which Android does not let other apps access, and it is not uploaded to cloud backup. The app has no servers or accounts of its own that could suffer a breach, and it receives no data from the internet other than ads. The file you export is created in a private app folder and only leaves it when you share it. Google's ads SDK connections are encrypted (TLS). Messages you send us are kept in an email account only JR Soft can access, and deleted within the periods above.

Your rights

If you live in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, the GDPR (or your country's equivalent law) gives you the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection. In practice, since this app keeps no data linked to you outside your phone:

  • Access and portability: everything the app stores can be seen in it (History, Health and the charger test), and you can take it with you using "Export history (CSV)".
  • Rectification and erasure: you can change or remove the design capacity, delete each charger test, clear all the app's data in Android settings or uninstall it. The advertising ID can be reset or deleted in Android settings.
  • Objection and withdrawing consent: Settings → Ad privacy options; and alerts, from the app itself or Android's notification settings.

Data collected by Google's library is managed by Google, and rights over it are exercised with Google: policies.google.com/privacy. You can also complain to the Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD) or to your country's data protection authority (GDPR, Art. 77).

US residents

If you live in a US state with its own privacy law (for example California, Colorado, Connecticut, Texas or Virginia), you have the right to ask that your personal data not be sold or shared for targeted advertising. You can do it in the app: Settings → Ad privacy options → the option not to sell or share your data. From then on you will see non-personalised or limited ads. Your battery readings, charges and settings stay on your phone and are not sent to anyone; for ads, Google's SDK uses device identifiers and an approximate location (city level). For any other request about your data, write to us at demual@gmail.com.

You can also ask us what data of yours we hold, to delete it or to correct it, without being treated worse for doing so. In the last 12 months, the categories Google's SDK may have collected for advertising are identifiers (device and advertising), app activity (interactions with ads) and an approximate location inferred from the IP address; no sensitive data is collected. JR Soft only keeps the emails you send it. This policy is reviewed at least once a year.

Residents of Brazil

If you live in Brazil, the General Data Protection Law (LGPD, Law 13,709/2018) gives you, among others, the rights of confirmation and access, correction, anonymisation or deletion, portability, information about who your data is shared with and revocation of consent (Art. 18). As JR Soft keeps no data of yours outside your phone, they are exercised as explained in "Your rights"; those over advertising data, with Google, which may process it outside Brazil with its own safeguards. Advertising is based on the legitimate interest in funding a free app (Art. 7, IX). You can write to us at demual@gmail.com or complain to the ANPD (Autoridade Nacional de Proteção de Dados).

Children

Aguantín is meant for adults and is not directed at anyone under 18. We do not knowingly collect data from minors; the technical data AdMob sends is described above.

What Aguantín is not

"Battery health" refers to the phone's battery, not to your health: Aguantín has no health features. Capacities, cycles, current and forecasts are estimates based on what each phone reports, not manufacturer measurements. Samsung and Pixel are trademarks of their owners; Aguantín only explains where their settings are and is not affiliated with them.
